How ATS Machines Actually Work

We simulate the behavior of applicant tracking systems from the early 2000s — still used by 75%+ of Fortune 500 companies.

What they can't read

Images, columns, tables, text boxes, headers/footers, PDFs with embedded graphics, special fonts, and smart punctuation.

How they parse

Top to bottom, left to right, plain text only. They look for exact section headers and keyword matches. No context, no inference.

What scores you higher

Single-column layout, plain text, standard section headers, parseable email/phone, no tables or images, safe fonts.

What is an Applicant Tracking System (ATS)?

An Applicant Tracking System is software used by employers to collect, sort, scan, and rank job applications. Many popular platforms like Workday, Taleo, Greenhouse, and Lever utilize parsing technology that strips resumes of formatting and searches for text content, headings, and keywords. If your resume employs complex formatting—such as tables, multi-columns, graphics, or unusual fonts—the ATS might fail to extract vital information, resulting in an automatic rejection or a poor ATS score. Top ATS Best Practices

  • Stick to standard fonts (Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman).
  • Use explicit headings (Experience, Education, Skills).
  • Avoid tables, headers/footers, and text boxes.
  • Save and upload files in standard formats (PDF or DOCX).

How to Improve Your ATS Score
